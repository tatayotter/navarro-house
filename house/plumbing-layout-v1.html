Navarro House plumbing layout

Draft v5, 19 Nov (decided: new septic tank; kitchen and ground T&B on city water; tank with an auto-stop pump for the rest). All our waste goes to our own new septic tank under the car bay; the old tank under the kitchen is shared with both neighbours, so it stays for them and we cap our lines. The kitchen and ground T&B run on city water; everything else comes from a tank in the car bay through a pump that only runs while a tap is open. Plans have the street (southwest) at the top, like the electrical layout, so left and right are swapped compared with standing in the street. Valve positions and heights are on the family site page "Water valves". Positions are a first draft: your plumber and engineer set the final ones.

Why this route

  • The roof T&B sits right above the stairwell void, so its stack drops straight down the open void to the ground. It needs only one core through the roof slab and none through the 2nd floor slab.
  • The same corner chase carries the vent and the water riser to the roof, boxed in behind the end of the pantry counter.
  • The ground floor is being re-tiled anyway, so the main line goes in a trench under the new floor: ground T&B, under the hollow old stair, through the dining area, to the car bay.
  • The septic tank sits at the front, so the desludging truck reaches it from the street and the outflow pipe is short: it reuses the existing sidewalk pipe that leads to the main basin at the corner street (Rowil 20 Nov). The plumber checks that pipe is clear and sound before connecting.

Water: city water downstairs, tank + pump for the rest

  • Supply: the city line comes in under the kitchen sink (meter is past the annex-side neighbour). The kitchen and the ground T&B stay on direct city water (pressure downstairs is good enough), through a check valve inside the kitchen sink cabinet. The line continues in the dining ceiling to the tank, and a float valve fills it.
  • Where: 1,000 L tank (about 1.05 m across, 1.5 m tall, about 1 t full) on a plinth at the partition end of the panel-side car bay wall, with the pump in front of it, about 0.9 m clear of the panel. The walkway beside the car stays about 1.1 m wide, and the tank stays off the septic and the inspection chamber. Both stand about 5 cm off the party wall. The partition's wood door sits in the walkway just beside the tank, hinged on the window side, and swings into the dining side, so it never hits the tank or blocks the crossover valve.
  • Pump: 0.5 HP constant-pressure inverter pump. It starts when a tap opens and stops by itself when every fixture is closed (pressure and flow sensing), with dry-run protection. No pressure-tank clicking, and it only runs while someone uses water.
  • Low noise: rubber anti-vibration pads, flexible hoses on both pump ends, a vented box lined with acoustic foam, pipe clips with rubber inserts, nothing bolted to the party wall.
  • On the pump: roof T&B, rooftop faucet, terrace tap (washer + hose) and the car bay hose bib (on the partition between the window and the door, car side, at 0.6 m; its pipe crosses above the door at ceiling height; for washing the car; that water goes to the street, not the septic). Washing the car doesn't drop the pressure downstairs.
  • Crossover valve (normally closed) between the pump line and the ground floor line, with a check valve, at 1.2 m on the wall just inside the partition (dining side, by the stairwell), so you can reach it without opening the ceiling. During rotational water schedules, open it and the tank feeds the kitchen and ground T&B too; close it when city water returns. The tank refills by itself. A brownout doesn't affect the ground floor.
  • Every valve within reach: main shut-off and the ground-floor check valve in the kitchen sink cabinet; crossover at 1.2 m on the wall; pump valves and check valve inside the pump box (lift-off lid); angle valves under each fixture; terrace faucet shut-off on the exposed car bay ceiling pipe (step stool); rooftop faucet shut-off beside the faucet. No valve sits above the PVC ceiling, so no access hatch is needed.
  • Electrical: dedicated 20 A circuit with RCD, weatherproof outlet at 1.2 m on the wall beside the pump. Sent to the electrical plan.

T&B layouts: keep the shower away from the door

  • Rule used in both: the shower goes in the corner diagonally opposite the door, the toilet sits between them, and the lavatory stays on the door side. The door never faces the spray.
  • Ground T&B (about 1.84 × 1.42 m): door at the front right, shower zone about 0.85 × 1.0 m in the back left corner, toilet in the middle of the back wall, compact lavatory on the front wall beside the door.
  • Roof T&B (about 1.60 × 1.70 m): door at the back of the inner wall, shower zone about 1.0 × 0.9 m in the front outer corner (under the high window, next to the stack and the exhaust fan), toilet on the outer wall near the door, lavatory on the front wall's inner corner.
  • Shower head: a ceiling or wall-arm rain head that falls straight down, plus a handheld on a slide bar. Avoid a fixed head that sprays sideways toward the room.
  • Keep water in the shower zone: slope the floor 1–2% to the shower drain, and make the shower zone 2–3 cm lower than the rest of the floor (or a 5 cm tiled curb). No glass screen needed.
  • Door: waterproof PVC or aluminium door with a small threshold. Ground T&B door can swing out into the passage so it never sits wet inside.
  • Optional: a ceiling-track shower curtain (about ₱1–2k) if guests splash a lot.

Pipes and septic tank

  • Drain and vent: thick PVC sanitary pipe (Series 1000) under the slab and for the stack. Main 100 mm at a 2% fall (2 cm per metre); branches 50 mm; vents 50 mm.
  • Cleanouts at the start of the main, at the stack foot and at each bend; the inspection chamber where the lines meet.
  • Water: PPR, 25 mm (¾″) main from the pump, up the stairwell chase to the roof, 20 mm (½″) to fixtures. Ground floor lines run in the hung ceilings and drop in the walls; the car bay stretch is painted surface pipe next to the electrical conduit.
  • Septic: 2 chambers, inside about 1.20 × 2.40 m and 1.2–1.5 m liquid depth (enough for about 8–10 people; plumber sizes it to the code). Watertight walls, car-rated cover slab, 2 sealed manholes in the walkway beside the car, not under the wheels.
  • No trap between the house and the septic, so the tank vents up through the stack. Plumber confirms if a separate tank vent is also needed.
  • Terrace hose faucet: ½″ PPR tees off the pump main in the car bay (with a shut-off valve), runs as a painted surface pipe on the car bay ceiling kept about 0.6 m away from the panel wall, then goes up through a small core (about 32 mm) in the 2nd floor slab right under the faucet. Short, few joints, and all visible. The core gets a sleeve sealed against water on the terrace side; engineer marks it clear of the wall beam. Pumped pressure, so it works like the ground floor taps.
  • Terrace washing machine (decided 20 Nov): it stands against the panel-side terrace wall behind the tap, on a tiled tray with a 5 cm curb and a floor drain, so spills and a burst hose stay in the tray. The tap becomes a dual-outlet tap at 1.0 m (washer hose + garden hose). The washer's drain hose goes into a 50 mm standpipe with a trap. The standpipe and the tray drain join a 50 mm pipe that runs along the wall base in a small tiled channel to the front, through the front sill wall, and down the facade on the panel-side pier. At grade it empties into the street drain (Rowil 20 Nov), with a cleanout at the foot. No new slab core and nothing near the panel.
  • Rain downspouts and the hose bib stay separate and go to the street. Never into the septic.
  • Roof room window AC (front wall): run its condensate in a small 16–20 mm hose to the nearest roof slab drain, so it doesn't drip and stain the open slab.
  • Old septic tank (under the kitchen) is SHARED with both neighbours, so it stays in service for them. We only disconnect and cap our own lines into it. Agree in writing how they reach its manhole for desludging, since it is under our kitchen.

Check with your engineer and plumber

  • Structural Septic tank under the car bay: cover slab and walls designed for the car's wheel load. Digging about 1.8 m deep near the party wall and the front columns can undermine footings; locate them first, the engineer sets the tank position.
  • Structural Small core (about 32 mm) through the 2nd floor slab for the terrace faucet line, near the front corner on the panel side: engineer marks it clear of the wall beam.
  • Structural Core through the roof slab for the stack (about 150 mm): engineer marks it clear of the beam on the front column line and the party-wall beam.
  • Structural Raised floor and wet walls of the roof T&B add load on the roof slab (already flagged for the room walls). Waterproof the T&B floor before tiling.
  • Venting: ground T&B and kitchen vent runs through the hung ceiling to the stairwell chase and joins the stack above the roof T&B fixtures. If that is too long, the plumber may accept air admittance valves instead.
  • Septic outflow reuses the existing sidewalk pipe to the corner basin: have the plumber rod or camera it first. Ask the city what permit the new septic tank needs.
  • Water tank: opaque food-grade tank with a sealed lid; clean it once a year. Keep it and the pump clear of the septic manholes and the panel.

Questions for you

  1. Where does the ground T&B drain run now (into the old tank under the kitchen)? If its pipe heads the right way, the plumber can reuse the T&B end of it.
  2. Car side: the septic is in the walkway beside the car, and the tank and pump are at the partition end of the panel-side wall. If the car parks on the other side, I mirror it.
