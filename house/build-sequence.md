### The labour anchor (30–40% of materials)

**Rule (set 20 Nov):** for every hired job, labour is budgeted at **30–40% of the materials that job uses**, the common local labour markup. The low end of each range uses 30% of the low materials figure, the high end 40% of the high one. It is a **negotiation basis**, not a price to offer: open below 30% and try to settle there; 40% is the most to accept.

- **Materials** means the plan pages' estimates with their own labour lines taken out, so labour is never counted twice.
- **Supplier-installed and equipment** items are priced by the shop with installation included (glass and aluminium, the roll-up shutter, the ceiling package, curtains, core drilling), or are machines (pump, aircon). No labour markup is added to them; negotiate them as one installed price.
- **Example:** the stair welder agreed ₱7,000, which is 27% of the ₱26,284 stair materials, below even the 30% end (₱7,885) and well under 40% (₱10,514).
- When a real quote comes in, write it next to the anchor in the phase and change its tag to Local.

### One crew per trade, one visit each

- **Ceilings:** every hung ceiling in the house is done **in one go** by one crew (step 5.4): PVC in the bedroom, living room, kitchen and dining, gypsum on the terrace and in the rooftop room. One mobilisation saves labour.
- **Partitions and double walls:** **one supplier** does all framed partition and double-wall work in one job (step 5.3): the rooftop room's insulation and Hardiflex lining, the rooftop T&B framed fibre-cement, the terrace frontage inner wall, and the bedroom Wall 1 double wall. Before leaving, they **set the provisions for the glass and door installers**: framed openings, backing and fixing points.
- **Glass and doors:** every window, slider and door is installed **in one go** after that (step 5.5). Until then the rooftop openings are closed temporarily with plywood or tarp.

**Demolition** is labour only, so the 30–40% rule has nothing to anchor to: get it quoted as a lump sum (Online guesses are in Phase 1). **Not costed yet:** the electrical permit, the annex barn door. The ground T&B tiles and fixtures are estimated at the same rate as the rooftop T&B until it is measured. The master bedroom total includes its own ceiling, wiring and double wall, which also appear in Phases 4 and 5, so the grand total overlaps by roughly ₱20k–40k.

## Phase 1: Demolition contract and the steel stair

**Starts when:** now. **Unlocks:** safe access to the rooftop for Phase 2, and every opening the later phases need.
**Why demolition comes first:** cutting the slab over a finished stair would drop concrete and dust on new treads and paint, and the stair room is easier to prop and work in while it's empty. So the slab is cut first, and the rest of the demolition is bundled into the **same contract**: one crew, one price, two short visits. (The cut is at the bottom end of the opening, over step 3. The stair's top tie is on the other edge, at the top end, so the cut doesn't touch it.)

**Slab cut size: about 20 cm.** Headroom over step 3 is about 182 cm now. Cutting the edge back moves it over a lower step, so each 1 cm cut adds about 0.94 cm of headroom (19.2 cm rise per 20.5 cm tread). 20 cm gives **about 200 cm**, which clears a 5′10″ (178 cm) person by about 22 cm, enough to walk up comfortably without ducking, and matches the usual 2.0 m stair headroom. 10 cm would give about 191 cm, 15 cm about 196 cm. Cutting more than about 20 cm gains nothing, because the beam over step 1 already limits headroom to about 199 cm. ✅ **The engineer has approved the slab cutout (20 Nov).** Cut to the approved size and edge detail.

**Cost:** phase budget **₱64,334–100,884** before contingency. **Every demolition price includes touch-up and finishing:** cut edges plastered and squared, rebar ends treated, debris hauled, ready for the next trade.
